What began as a push for faster, easier transactions has now grown into a movement that 
empowers every citizen, shopkeeper, and business to participate in India’s digital-first 
future.

consumers. Nowhere else is the power of inclusion, trust, and innovation more evident 
than in India, a nation that has arguably become the world’s payments laboratory, 
continually setting new benchmarks in access, reliability, and security.

much more than just convenience. It is actively powering the formalisation of GDP, helping 
merchants and consumers alike transition into the digital economy, and fostering an 
environment where transparency, scale, and operational agility are within everyone’s 
reach. 
Small and micro-merchants, once limited by geography or legacy systems, now operate on 
the same rails as major enterprises, thanks to the simplicity and universality of solutions 
such as QR codes and app-based platforms. 
Policy and innovation have become the dual forces driving India’s global edge, creating 
population-scale trust and resilience.

Actually a UPI Superpower
While cards and PoS continue to expand steadily, the real story lies in UPI's shifting 
transaction patterns, where falling values mask a profound transformation in consumer 
behaviour.
Over the last 18 months, UPI's trajectory has been transformative. Transaction volumes 
climbed from 12.2 billion in January 2024 to 18.4 billion by June 2025. In 1H 2025 alone, 
volumes surged 35% year-on-year to 106.36 billion, while values rose 23% to ₹143.34 
trillion.
The Power of Small
The most striking shift is the decline in average ticket size from ₹1,478 in 1H 2024 to 
₹1,348 in 1H 2025. This is not a weakness; it is UPI's superpower. Smaller spends at 
kiranas, delivery platforms, mobility services, and utilities demonstrate how QR codes have 
normalized as the default payment mode.

Tiny Taps Beat One Big Swipe
India’s UPI engine is powered by two primary flows /uni2013.case P2P transfers and P2M payments. In 
1H 2025, P2P transactions grew 31% to 39.35 billion, while P2M transactions surged 37%

food and beverage, and utilities have normalized QR pay. The shift compressed average 
ticket sizes but expanded the system’s reach.
The P2M surge is structural, not temporary. What once relied on cash or cards is now 
digital by default at kirana stores and neighborhood merchants. This “kirana effect” proves 
that ubiquity is built on countless small-value transactions rather than a few large swipes.

advanced 26% to ₹39.48 trillion. Hyper-local spends are driving frequency and stickiness. 
For consumers, digital is now a habit; for merchants, it delivers low-cost acceptance that 
builds trust and liquidity simultaneously.

India’s UPI ecosystem looks app-driven, but true performance is decided deeper in the 
stack. Consumers see familiar brands like PhonePe, Google Pay, or Paytm, yet the 
reliability of every transaction depends on the banks that move money in the background. 
This duality, apps for reach, banks for resilience, defines UPI’s success today.
Falling Ticket Sizes, Rising Volumes
The average ticket size of UPI transactions fell 9% in 1H 2025, from ₹1,478 to ₹1,348. P2P 
ATS declined 7%, while P2M fell 8% to ₹589. This trend reflects UPI’s expanding role in 
daily micro-spends, such as groceries, mobility, and utilities, where frequency and trust 
matter more than value.
Front-End Concentration
By June 2025, PhonePe, Google Pay, and Paytm controlled more than 90% of both volume 
and value. While this gives the impression of app-led dominance, the real bottlenecks—or 
strengths—come from the banking partners that power these transactions.

Net Banking in a UPI World
Net Banking rarely makes headlines, but it continues to underpin India’s financial 
backbone. In 1H 2025, volumes dipped slightly to 2.22 billion, down 1% YoY. Yet values 
surged 30% to ₹717.4 trillion, underscoring how Net Banking remains the channel of choice 
for high-value payments.
The story lies in its users. Corporations, treasuries, and government agencies rely on Net 
Banking for tax payments, GST remittances, and large-ticket treasury flows. This explains 
why the average ticket size jumped to ₹313,954 in 1H 2025, up from ₹239,421 a year 
earlier—by far the highest among all payment modes.
This dual profile “flat volumes, explosive values” reflects Net Banking’s niche. For 
consumers, UPI dominates; for enterprises, Net Banking moves the real money. The base 
is now so large that growth will look modest in percentage terms, but the absolute value 
gains will keep compounding.
Looking ahead, Net Banking’s trajectory will remain stable. Its role as the rail for corporate 
and regulatory settlements ensures longevity, even as retail adoption consolidates under 
UPI and cards. In India’s digital payments mix, Net Banking is the invisible giant – less 
visible, but indispensable.
